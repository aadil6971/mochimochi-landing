    How it's made

    From rice to frozen in four steps.

    1. 01

      Rice

      Short-grain glutinous rice, soaked overnight and steamed until every grain is glossy and sticky. It smells like warm porridge.

      Steamed for 40 minutes
    2. 02

      Mochi

      While it is still hot, the rice is pounded into one smooth, stretchy dough. We turn it and pound it again, until it pulls like warm taffy.

      Pounded by hand and machine
    3. 03

      Filling

      A scoop of ice cream goes in the middle of a thin disc of dough. We pinch it shut in one motion, before anything has a chance to melt.

      Wrapped in under 10 seconds
    4. 04

      Frozen

      Each ball drops into a blast freezer and sets hard in an hour. Then it is packed cold, so the dough stays soft and the cream stays cold.

      -25°C blast freeze

      Questions

      Things people ask us.

      Something missing? Write to hello@mochimochi.example and a real person answers.

      We pack your mochi at -25°C in a foam shell with two frozen cold packs. A courier brings it the next day, and the box stays properly cold for up to 72 hours. When it arrives, put the mochi straight in your freezer.

      Take it out of the freezer and wait five to eight minutes. The dough goes soft and stretchy, the ice cream stays cold. Eat it with your hands. Do not put it back once it has softened.

      Three months in a freezer at -18°C or colder. The best-before date is printed on the tray. After that they are still safe, but the dough starts to dry.

      Every mochi is gluten-free and vegetarian. All flavours contain milk. Black sesame contains sesame and ube contains coconut. We pack in a kitchen that also handles soy and tree nuts, so we cannot promise a nut-free box.
